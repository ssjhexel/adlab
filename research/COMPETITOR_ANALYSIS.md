# Competitor Creative Analysis — Pet Itch / Skin Ads (Meta)

**Scope:** the 12 videos in `competitor_ads/`. Each was reviewed at the frame level: contact sheets at 1 fps for the full ad, plus 3 fps for the first 3 seconds (`research/contact_sheets/`). Each also has a timestamped transcript (`research/transcripts/`), automated cut detection, and an audio-level check.

**Ground rules for this document**
- **Observation** = directly visible or audible in the files, or counted.
- **Hypothesis** = our inference about *why* something might work. We do not have the competitors' Meta data: no spend, ROAS, CTR, CPA, hook rate or run dates. Nothing here claims any ad "won".
- The files are 360×360 1:1 downloads, so native aspect ratio and full resolution are unknown.

---

## 1. The set at a glance

## 2. Ad-by-ad breakdown (spray ads first)

### C01 — LPP spray: "Spray on the reddened areas"
- **0–3s:** Frame 0 is *mid-spray*. A hand mists a golden retriever's belly covered in red papules, with the bottle and label in frame. At 1.7s it cuts to a circular hairless patch on a dog's back. A glowing orange ring appears, and the patch visibly fades to calm skin by 2.7s.
- **VO opener:** imperative list ("Spray on the reddened areas, spray on the hairless areas, spray where there are scabs…").
- **Body:** scratching/biting behaviors, then a **UV flashlight "invisible bugs"** reveal with red "!!!" graphics. Then a **human forearm with bites**: "Don't wait for your family to suffer as well." Then "made with plant extracts so it doesn't matter if your dog licks it."
- **Claims:** "quick relief and anti-itching in just 8 seconds", "inflammation recovers within a week".
- **Offer:** bundle box shot, "BUY 2 GET 1 + FREE SHIPPING", "Stock is limited". It closes on two happy dogs with the bottle.
- **Note:** the narrative jumps topic several times (skin → bugs → family → lick-safe → speed). It works as a run of hooks more than a story.

### C03 — LPP spray: "Part the fur and look underneath" (60s)
- **0–3s:** A woman's hands (rings, blue knit) rest on a border collie's black coat, with a faint red glow visible through the fur. At 1.7s her fingers part the coat, revealing a vivid red, pebbled patch.
- **VO:** "If your pet keeps licking the same spot, part the fur and look underneath."
- **Product at 7s** held to camera: "Now watch what happens…". Then a **macro 3D skin surface** where the mist spreads and "the redness visibly begins to calm".
- **Reframe:** "Tail licking, scratching, head shaking and rubbing are not always bad habits… your pet is trying to relieve discomfort you cannot see."
- **Demo:** "Separate the fur and spray it onto the affected skin", with a glowing particle VFX as "the mist reaches beneath the coat", then "without thick creams or rubbing a tender spot".
- **Transformation:** rough surface smooths, color normalizes, "the coat begins to fill in again" (macro of fur refilling).
- **Outcome:** stops licking, sleeps peacefully, enjoys touch, plays (dogs running in a garden).
- **CTA:** "Keep pet skin spray ready before a small irritated spot becomes a bigger problem." No explicit offer.

### C10 — LPP spray: "Watch this red irritated patch calm down" (67s)
- **0–3s:** Frame 0 shows the spray nozzle already aimed at a large red patch on a golden, with a second hand parting the fur. At 2.3s a **glowing blue ring** expands from the patch centre ("calm down"), as if the product is "activating".
- **Body:** behaviors, then "that's why I keep pet skin spray within reach" (first person). Part fur and spray. **3D cell cross-section** goes from red, cracked cells with lightning-like fissures to blue, hydrated cells ("support the skin's natural protective barrier"). Then "no thick cream… no messy residue". Long healing sequence. A wide lesion catalogue: belly, ear flap, paw, French bulldog face, white poodle.
- **End:** dog, French bulldog and cat beside the bottle, red animated arrows, "Tap below".
- C03, C10 and C11 share a script skeleton and phrases ("part the fur", "lightweight mist reaches the skin beneath the coat", "keep … nearby").

### C11 — LPP spray: UGC-style variant of the C03/C10 script
- **0–3s:** A chocolate Lab looks up at camera on a living-room rug: "If your pet keeps licking the same spot, don't ignore it." At 2s fingers part dark fur to reveal a small pink spot.
- More "real home" texture: handheld, daylight, varied owners (woman, couple, older woman), multi-breed plus cat lineup, travel case. An "ITCH ALERT" red title card appears late.
- **Offer:** Halloween set (pumpkins, candles), "Buy 2 Get 1 Free, plus free shipping", "tap the shopping cart below".

### C04 — LPP spray: first-person testimonial
- **0–3s:** A golden scratching its ear with a hind leg, with a big red "**ITCH ALERT**" title. At 3s the product appears with "BUY 2, GET 1 FREE" (the offer is shown at 3 seconds).
- **VO:** "My dog used to scratch and lick his paws all the time and honestly, it was heartbreaking to watch…" Spray demos on several breeds, "no bath, no rinsing", "allergy season", travel kit.
- **Offer:** Halloween bundle card, B2G1 + free shipping.

### C06 — LPP spray: paw-focused, "One spray in the morning…"
- **0–3s:** Frame 0 shows a backlit mist hitting a raw, swollen paw held in a hand. Cut to a dog lying on carpet being sprayed on the paw. VO promise: "…those swollen itchy paws will finally stop bothering your dog."
- **Body:** extreme **macro of cracked, inflamed interdigital skin** ("likely an infection in their paw pads… left untreated it will only get worse"). **Blue frost-particle VFX** for "cools the inflamed skin". **3D cell animation** contrasts "temporary quick fixes that mask the itch while thinning the skin" (implied steroid jab) with "calms inflammation at the source… rebuilds the natural skin barrier".
- **Safety demo:** a French bulldog licks its treated paw: "completely safe even if they lick it". **Split-screen before/after paws.** Sleeping dog.
- **Offer:** "It's the last day for this deal. Buy two get one free."

### C07 / C08 — LPP spray: "Pet Itch Relief Spray — fast soothing care in just 8 seconds"
- Near-identical scripts (two edits of one concept).
- **C08 0–3s:** two fingers rest on a brown dog's coat beside a pink bald spot. A spray passes and **the spot fades and hair grows in within ~2.5 seconds** while the VO says the product name and "8 seconds".
- **C07:** the same idea on curly dark fur. At 11–14s a red patch fades and grey hair fills in ("notice calmer skin in just 7 days").
- **Gentleness proof:** mist sprayed onto an open palm ("color-free, odor-free"), a child hugging a bichon ("great choice for families with children"), product + box hero.
- **Offer:** B2G1 + free shipping, "click the shopping cart below".

### Supplement ads (structure reference only)
- **C02 (probiotic chews):** golden licking a red paw at frame 0. "Thousands of dogs are silently suffering… most owners have no idea." X-ray dog, glowing gut, yeast macros. A **vet silhouette with a shield icon**. **"Topical treatments only mask surface symptoms."** That is a direct attack on our category.
- **C05 (probiotic chews):** Pixar-style bichon with tear stains. Dog-POV narration ("How does a dog ask for help without words?"). Cartoon gut battle.
- **C09 (joint powder):** happy dog bounding on a couch: "Have you noticed your furry friend who *used to* jump around easily now hesitates…" A past-versus-present contrast hook.
- **C12 (probiotic chews):** listicle ("four key signs of yeast"). The most authentic-looking footage in the set: real phone clips, a "70% of immune system" stat, and a personal claim ("My two dogs suffered… after two months, both pups finally feel comfortable").

**Other observations**
- **Variant clustering.** The 8 LPP ads reduce to about 3 script skeletons: (a) C03/C10/C11 "part the fur / keep it nearby", (b) C07/C08 "8 seconds", (c) C01/C06 aggressive-claim. C04 is a testimonial variant. Each skeleton has multiple edits with swapped hooks, breeds, seasonal end cards and VO pacing.
- **Lesion art direction.** Lesions are hyper-saturated, evenly textured and often geometrically clean (round or oval patches with crisp edges). They read instantly at thumbnail size, but they are one of the clearest "AI" tells in the set.
- **Brand absence.** No LPP ad says the brand name, names an ingredient (beyond "plant extracts" or "gentle formula"), cites an expert, shows a review or star rating, or explains why this spray is different. The product is effectively a commodity.
- **Claim aggressiveness.** C01, C06, C07 and C08 use time-bound or medical-sounding claims: "8 seconds", "within a week", "7 days", "infection", "heals instead of covering up", "rebuilds the skin barrier".
- **Unsafe-looking demos (vs. our label).** Spraying ear flaps close to the canal (C10, C08) and licking of treated paws (C06) appear. Our label prohibits the inside of ears and advises a collar for lickers.
- **Category attack from supplements.** C02 tells viewers that "topical treatments only mask surface symptoms". Topical-spray buyers seeing these ads will have heard that objection.

## 4. Hypotheses: why this structure may be working

*These cannot be confirmed without performance data. The variant clustering above is the best proxy available: advertisers usually duplicate and re-edit structures they believe are profitable.*

- **H1 — Revulsion plus relief is the hook engine.** A raw lesion on a beloved animal in frame 0 triggers an immediate protective and disgust response, which stops the scroll. A hand is already "fixing" it (P1 + P2 + P3), so the tension starts resolving inside 3 seconds. The viewer gets problem, product and promise before deciding to skip.
- **H2 — "Part the fur" is a participatory hook.** It hands the owner an action ("go check your dog") and implies hidden, unseen suffering, which adds guilt and curiosity. It also creates a natural before-state for the transformation. It appears in 4 ads across 2 different script skeletons, the most-reused hook device in the set.
- **H3 — Visible transformation is the core proof.** There are no reviews, stars or experts in any spray ad. The before → after on the same patch carries all the persuasion. The 1–3 second "instant" version is the most attention-grabbing, and also the least believable and most claim-risky.
- **H4 — The behavior reframe enlarges the audience.** "Licking, head shaking, rubbing aren't just habits" converts owners who did not think their pet had a "skin problem".
- **H5 — Ease plus gentleness clears the final objections** ("will it be a hassle?" / "is it safe around my kids and cat?") right before the offer.
- **H6 — Offer conditioning.** B2G1 + free shipping is so uniform that category buyers may now expect a bundle. Recurring use ("ongoing skin care") justifies multi-bottle offers.
- **H7 — Captions plus literal visuals make the ad work sound-off,** and VO at ~200 wpm leaves no dead air.

## 5. Gaps and weaknesses we can exploit

1. **No reason to believe.** The only proof is the instant transformation, which a skeptical, been-burned owner may disbelieve. Atokong has a real authority stack: a vet with 20+ years, a 30-year immune-cell researcher, PITEN biotech and named Korean botanicals.
2. **Commodity product, no brand.** "Pet skin spray" is interchangeable. The Korean skincare halo (K-beauty's reputation for advanced, gentle formulation) is a ready-made premium frame that none of these ads can claim.
3. **The "AI look" comes from the CG.** Glow rings, frost particles, cartoon cells and geometric lesions are where the competitor ads look most synthetic. Realism wins by replacing those with *photographic* devices: macro mist droplets, a locked-off daily photo series, dermatoscope-style close-ups.
4. **Believability gap on speed.** We can show what is plausible: *immediate behavioral relief* (the dog stops scratching and settles) and *progressive skin and coat improvement over days*, with day counters. This also matches our brief's claim structure exactly ("rapid itch relief… visible improvement over continued use").
5. **Steroid anxiety goes unanswered.** C06 hints at "quick fixes that thin the skin". Atokong can state "non-steroidal" plainly as a fact, without disparaging anyone.
6. **The "topicals just mask symptoms" objection.** Our skin-barrier and skin-immunity R&D story answers it implicitly.

## 6. Implications for the Atokong ad

**Keep (structural table stakes)**
- Lesion + hand + "part the fur" inside the first 2 seconds. Product visible by ~3s in at least one hook variant.
- A cut every ~2s, dense first-person VO (~170–190 wpm), word-by-word captions, literal VO-to-picture mapping.
- Behavior reframe → mechanism → easy demo → transformation → emotional payoff → offer.
- Bundle offer + free shipping on the end card (pending the client's offer).

**Beat**
- Replace CG mechanism shots with **authority shots** (vet hands, lab, botanicals) and **photographic proof** (macro mist, day-counter photo series).
- Make the brand and the Korean-dermatology story *the* differentiator, said out loud and shown.
- A transformation timeline the brand can stand behind ("Day 1 → Day 21"), labelled "Dramatization".

**Avoid**
- "X seconds", "heals", "infection", "safe if licked", spraying in or near ears, eyes or mouth, showing licking of a freshly treated spot, glowing rings and cartoon cells.
